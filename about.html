What counts as sourced on this site

  • The sourcing rules, in full

    These rules are applied to every article before publication, and they are the reason this site is worth reading: a number is treated as an unproven claim until its source is documented.

    • Every number carries a source. A figure with no attributable origin is removed rather than softened with hedging language.
    • Sample size is stated when one exists. Where a survey reports a sample, the sample is named in the sentence that uses the number.
    • The time window is stated. Measurement periods are given explicitly, so a reader can tell a one-week reading from a quarter-long trend.
    • Estimates are labelled as estimates. Forecasts, vendor indices, relayed figures and secondary reports are marked as such in the sentence where they appear.
    • Conflicting figures are shown, not averaged away. When two credible sources disagree, both numbers are printed with their sources and the disagreement is named instead of being silently resolved.
    • Vendor claims stay vendor claims. A proprietary index or a self-reported traffic number is attributed to the vendor and is never presented as an independent finding.
    • Primary announcements are read directly. Platform change logs and official documentation are retrieved first-hand rather than through a summary article.
    • Each article ends with a verification note stating what was checked, what was only reachable second-hand, and the publication date.

How the site is maintained

  • Plain, inspectable publishing

    The site is a static build with no tracking, no analytics and no third-party embeds, so every page can be read exactly as it was published. Each page carries structured data, a canonical URL, and the whole site is indexed in sitemap.xml and llms.txt.

    Publication and modification dates are real rather than cosmetic: the date in the markup is the date the article was written, and the sitemap reflects the last substantive edit.
