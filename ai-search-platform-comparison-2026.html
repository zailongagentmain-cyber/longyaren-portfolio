1. Why "AI search" is seven markets, not one

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is usually sold as a single discipline: write for machines, get cited, track mentions. That framing works for a beginner week. It fails by the second quarter, because the platforms you are optimizing for do not buy from the same suppliers, do not use the same citation logic, and do not change on the same schedule.

The evidence for the fragmentation is quantitative, not rhetorical. Multi-engine citation studies run through 2026 keep finding that the cited-domain overlap between engine families sits in the low teens — roughly 11% in the widest sample — and that even Google's own two surfaces disagree with each other. AI Overviews citations overlap Google's traditional top ten only around 17%. In other words, if your brand is cited on ChatGPT, you have learned almost nothing about your citation odds on Gemini, and if you rank on Google you have barely touched Copilot, Perplexity or Claude.

There is a second, more practical reason fragmentation matters: the fix for a missing citation is different on every platform. When ChatGPT does not cite you, the cause is often that a web-fetchable page never answered the question in extractable form, or that your brand name is ambiguous in the model's memory. When Perplexity does not cite you, the cause is often freshness or a missing multi-source corroboration. When Doubao does not cite you, the cause is almost always that your content never appeared inside the ByteDance ecosystem at all. These require different work. Treating them as one project means you diagnose the wrong problem for months.

The rule that follows. Never report a single number called "AI visibility." Report a vector: mention rate per platform, citation share per platform, position per platform. If you cannot draw a bar per platform, you are not measuring AI search — you are measuring the one platform you happened to remember to open. The measurement design is set out in AI citation tracking in 2026, and the commercial translation is in GEO ROI and attribution in 2026.

3. Sourcing models: the taxonomy that explains everything else

The fastest way to understand 2026's AI search landscape is to stop thinking of it as a list of brands and start thinking of it as a list of procurement strategies. Every platform buys information in one of four broad ways, and the four ways produce predictably different citation behaviour.

Model A — Real-time crawl (freshness wins)

Perplexity is the clearest example. It runs its own crawler, indexes pages continuously, and heavily weights recency and multi-source corroboration. The practical signature is that a freshly published, well-structured page can be cited within days, and that an authoritative but stale page gets displaced. Community-sourced validation is also unusually strong here — roughly 46.7% of Perplexity's top-ten citations in the widest 2026 analysis came from Reddit, which tells you something about what Perplexity considers corroborated.

Model B — Own index plus knowledge graph (authority wins)

Google AI Overviews and Gemini sit here. AI Overviews draw candidates from Google's own ranked results and then extract an answer; Gemini adds grounding on top. The consequence is strategic rather than technical: there is no separate "AI Overviews optimization", because ranking well in traditional Google results is the prerequisite and the AI layer is extraction behaviour on top of it. The genuinely distinct investment here is topic-cluster coverage, since Google's query fan-out retrieves a page once per matching subquery.

Model C — Parameter memory first, search second (structure wins)

ChatGPT and, more sharply, Claude live here. Both models frequently answer from training data and only search when they judge that live or specific information would improve the answer. Claude in particular decides per query whether it needs the web at all, which means an unseen brand can be invisible on Claude even with a strong page — not because the page was rejected, but because the answer was never a search-shaped question. When these platforms do search, they reward structure: definition-shaped openers, self-contained FAQ answers, tables, and unambiguous entity naming.

Model D — Ecosystem-bound (membership wins)

This is the layer almost entirely absent from Western GEO discussion, and it is where Doubao, Yuanbao, Tongyi and, to a lesser degree, Kimi operate. The determining factor is usually not the quality of your page but whether your content exists inside a specific walled garden: Toutiao and Douyin for Doubao, WeChat official accounts for Yuanbao, Alibaba's aggregator and developer properties for Tongyi. In published 2026 measurements, Doubao's citations for travel and local-service questions came from Douyin content more than nine times in ten, and Yuanbao's WeChat official-account citations accounted for roughly a third of its total. A brand with no presence in those ecosystems does not have an optimization problem; it has a distribution problem, and no amount of on-site craft substitutes for it.

Why the taxonomy earns its keep. When a client says "AI isn't working for us", the first question is not "what page needs work" — it is "which procurement model governs the platform that is failing". Under Model A the answer is freshness and corroboration. Under Model B it is your existing Google position. Under Model C it is answer-shaped structure and entity clarity. Under Model D it is a publishing plan for someone else's platform. Diagnosing wrong here costs a quarter.

4. ChatGPT: parameter memory first, search fallback second

ChatGPT is the largest audience and the most frequently misread platform. The misconception is that ChatGPT "searches the web" the way a search engine does. It does not, at least not as its default behaviour. The model usually answers from what it learned, and searches when it needs live, specific or source-backed information — which is why a brand with strong offline awareness can appear in ChatGPT answers without any on-page work at all, and a brand with a flawless page can be absent.

Two documented facts shape the on-page work. First, OpenAI operates four agents with separate robots.txt controls: GPTBot for training, OAI-SearchBot for the search index that powers ChatGPT search answers, ChatGPT-User for user-triggered fetches, and the images agent. Blocking the training crawler while allowing the search crawler is a legitimate and common configuration; blocking the search crawler is what removes you from ChatGPT search answers. Second, in the 2026 measurement of 602 controlled prompts across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews/Gemini and Perplexity, ChatGPT cited the fewest sources of the three — 6.88 per answer against Perplexity's 16.35 and Google's 12.06 — yet produced the highest average influence score on the pages it did cite. That is the central ChatGPT fact: fewer citations, but the cited page does more work in the answer.

What this means operationally is that ChatGPT is not a traffic engine in the way Perplexity is, and it is not a paragraph-extraction engine. It is a place where being named once, correctly, inside a synthesized argument can be worth more than being cited eight times in a source list. The content that wins here is definition-shaped, unambiguous about the brand entity, and structured so that a clean claim can be attributed. That is also why entity SEO and knowledge graph authority pays off disproportionately on this platform: in a parameter-first model, name consistency is the entire game, because there is no ranking signal to lean on.

The weakness of ChatGPT as a target is measurement. There is no citation dashboard for site owners, so the only honest method is a fixed prompt panel logged over time — which is exactly the discipline described in AI citation tracking in 2026.

5. Perplexity: real-time crawl, freshness wins, transparency as a feature

Perplexity is the platform where the shortest path from publish to citation exists, and it is the platform where the smallest sites beat the largest brands most often. Three properties explain it.

It crawls its own index in real time. A page published today with a clean structure, visible date and a couple of corroborating sources can be cited within days — in the field, some teams report picks up within 48 hours of publication for high-authority sources, and Perplexity is repeatedly reported as the fastest of the major platforms to index new content.

It weights freshness and corroboration heavily. Multi-source agreement is a visible selection criterion: the same claim appearing in several independent, credible places raises its citation odds. That is why the "publish it once on your own site" strategy caps out quickly on Perplexity while a distributed strategy compounds.

It shows its work. Every answer lists its sources, which makes Perplexity the best natural laboratory in the market. You can run your own prompts, read the citations, and see exactly which domains the engine prefers for your topic — without any paid tool. That transparency is why Perplexity is the engine most often used as a monitoring dashboard by the teams doing serious multi-engine work.

The trade-off is scale. Perplexity's citation count per answer is the highest of the major platforms, but its user base and referral volume are smaller than ChatGPT's, so a citation there is worth less per impression. It also has no first-party reporting for site owners, and its user-triggered fetcher may act on a person's request regardless of robots.txt in some implementations, which complicates access-control reasoning. The permission detail is in AI crawler access control and log analysis in 2026; the platform's crawler identity and IP verification are in the multi-engine map at multi-engine AI search optimisation in 2026.

6. Google AI Overviews and Gemini: the index you already earned

Google is the only platform in this table where the answer to "how do I get cited by AI" is "rank normally". AI Overviews draw their candidate set from Google's own ranked results and then extract an answer from those candidates; Gemini adds grounding across Google's index, videos and forums on top. Google has stated publicly that no special markup is required for its AI features and that it does not need separate optimisation — no AI-specific schema switch, no special file. The honest reading is that ranking well is the optimisation, and answer-engine formatting is the lift on top.

Two practical consequences follow. First, if your traditional Google performance collapsed after the March 2026 core update, your AI Overviews visibility collapsed with it — the recovery path is the classic SEO recovery path, and it is documented in the March 2026 core update deep dive. Second, the distinct AI-era investment for Google is topic-cluster coverage rather than any new markup: Google's query fan-out rewrites one question into several parallel retrievals, and a page covering the cluster gets more retrieval attempts than one covering only the head term. That argument is developed in AI search topic clusters and content refresh in 2026, and the AI Overviews-specific mechanics are in the complete GEO guide.

Google is also the only major platform with first-party citation reporting for site owners: Search Console shipped a Generative AI report in June 2026, which means Google-engine visibility can be tracked with the same rigour as organic queries. Any platform comparison that ignores that reporting asymmetry is misleading about effort allocation.

The demotion that costs the most money. Both llms.txt and Schema Markup are routinely sold as AI citation levers. Google's own material states there is no separate AI index and that llms.txt is not used in Search; controlled tests on Schema Markup found citation effects that do not clear the noise floor against large control groups. Publish llms.txt because it is cheap and expresses your priorities, and keep Schema Markup accurate because it helps entity disambiguation and interpretation. Do not put either in a business case as a citation switch.

7. Microsoft Copilot: the cheapest engine to win

Copilot is the platform most often skipped and the one where the marginal cost of a win is lowest. It draws from the Bing index, so a site with existing Bing footing can be present in Copilot answers before writing a single new word. For organisations whose buyer persona lives in Microsoft tooling — enterprise, technical, procurement — Copilot can carry disproportionate commercial weight despite being a small share of consumer attention. The 2026 caveat that belongs in every comparison is that it is also the platform where the enterprise distribution channel sits: web search within Team and Enterprise workspaces can be toggled off by an owner, in which case Copilot answers come from training data only and your freshest pages are invisible regardless of quality.

Operationally, Copilot rewards verifiable, reference-style statements: numbers with dates, named standards, explicit comparisons. Microsoft also runs a specialist retrieval crawler, AzureAI-SearchBot, alongside Bingbot, which behaves far more selectively — a small number of hits rather than hundreds — so it should not be judged by volume in the logs. Bing Webmaster Tools ships an AI Performance report, which makes Copilot one of only two platforms in this table with first-party citation data.

8. Claude: selective search, primary sources, named authors

Claude is the outlier in the Western market and the easiest to misjudge, because its behaviour depends on a decision the user never sees: whether this particular question needs the web. Anthropic's web search runs on Anthropic's own infrastructure rather than a resold Google or Bing index, and it triggers selectively — on queries where live or specific information genuinely changes the answer — rather than defaulting to a broad multi-source search on every prompt.

Three documented operational facts make Claude the highest-leverage platform for B2B. First, Anthropic operates three independently controllable crawlers: ClaudeBot for training, Claude-SearchBot for search relevance — the one that gates citation eligibility — and Claude-User for user-triggered fetches. Blocking ClaudeBot does not block Claude-SearchBot, and a blanket wildcard rule catches all three at once, which is how teams accidentally disqualify themselves from Claude citations while believing they only opted out of training. Second, citations in the API carry the URL, title and up to 150 characters of cited text, which makes short self-contained answers the easiest thing to cite and a practical design constraint. Third, web fetch does not render JavaScript and only handles text, HTML and PDF, so any content that exists only after client-side rendering is invisible to it.

What Claude rewards, consistently across 2026 field testing, is depth over breadth, primary sources over aggregators, visible freshness indicators such as "last updated" dates, verifiable author credentials, and pages that link to the original research they describe. One growth lead summarised it usefully: Claude's citation count is roughly a fifth of ChatGPT's, and Claude will not usually hand you new users — it helps you keep the ones you have. If your business is enterprise, Claude is where existing users evaluate you; if it is consumer, ChatGPT is the battlefield.

One measurement note that belongs in any honest comparison: Anthropic publishes no citation dashboard for site owners. Claude Code's analytics track lines of code and API spend, not website citations. Measurement here means disciplined spot-checking.

Three findings from that data change strategy more than any single platform tactic.

DeepSeek is a first-tier citation engine that single-run testing under-counts. In one 2026 run it cited external content on 9 of 12 questions; with search explicitly enabled, it cited on 32 out of 32 samples. If you tested DeepSeek once and concluded it "doesn't cite the web", you drew the wrong conclusion from a real observation. This is the same trap as testing Claude on a query that does not need the web.

Kimi is mostly parameter-first and should be last. Nine of twelve answers were generated with no external citation at all, so content investment aimed at Kimi has the weakest measured pathway of the six. It is not that Kimi is bad; it is that its answers rarely change based on the live web for typical informational queries.

Zhihu is not the source people assume it is. In the domestic testing, Zhihu appeared almost only inside Wenxin Yiyan's citations. The genuinely high-frequency sources across engines were CSDN, Blog Garden, Sohu, NetEase, Toutiao and WeChat official accounts. If your domestic distribution plan is built around Zhihu because it feels like the community platform, it is aimed at the wrong pool.

Timing on the domestic layer follows the platform's crawl aggressiveness. Doubao is the fastest, typically surfacing high-authority published material within about 48 hours and showing first citations in roughly four to seven days. Yuanbao runs seven to fourteen days, with WeChat official accounts carrying roughly fifteen to twenty percent of its citations in public testing. Tongyi typically surfaces within ten to eighteen days, and DeepSeek is the slowest but the broadest — fifteen to thirty days, with the widest source coverage of the four. Two rules hold across all of them: high-authority media surfaces fastest and low-authority sites get "crawled but never cited", and cross-platform stable citation typically requires three to six months of sustained publishing rather than a single placement.

The common baseline that beats any platform-specific tactic. Every domestic engine, and most Western ones, weight authoritative news media consistently. Central and provincial news portals and head-of-vertical trade media behave as the stable, long-cited ballast across platforms — and a brand's own structured Q&A library is repeatedly cited once published, which is why owned content must be built as an extractable Q&A asset rather than as a blog feed.
One structural feature of the domestic layer. There is no single dominant gatekeeper index. Authority media is the shared ballast, and beyond it each engine reads its own ecosystem. The practical strategy is therefore not "pick one platform" but "publish the same core claim in four or five places, in the format each ecosystem prefers, with the facts aligned so cross-verification succeeds" — a distribution architecture rather than a content architecture. The content architecture side is where topic clusters earn their keep: a pillar page that states a position and a set of spokes that each prove it gives you both the depth that authority media wants to link to and the extractable fragments that engine-specific retrieval can lift.

10. Citation count is not citation weight

The most common analytical mistake in platform comparison is treating the number of citations as the metric. It is not, and our own 2026 research is unusually clear on this point.

Across 602 controlled prompts and 21,143 captured citations, the three platforms measured produced dramatically different citation densities — Perplexity 16.35 sources per answer, Google AI Overviews and Gemini 12.06, ChatGPT 6.88 — and yet ChatGPT's cited pages carried the highest average influence score, meaning the pages it did cite contributed more of the answer's substance. Citation count measures how loudly an engine advertises its sources; citation weight measures how much of its output is actually yours. A platform can cite you in a sources panel you were never mentioned in.

Three secondary findings from the same body of work refine the picture. Pages containing code blocks were cited with materially higher influence than pages without. Pages with significantly more words and more headings showed a marked influence advantage over pages with fewer of both. And the decomposition of citation probability into weighted signals — semantic relevance at roughly thirty to forty percent, source authority at twenty-five to thirty-five, content structure at fifteen to twenty, freshness at ten to fifteen, user engagement at five to ten — explains why a page that is well written but weakly sourced still underperforms, and why structure alone cannot rescue an authority gap.

The practical consequence for a comparison exercise: measure four numbers per platform, not one. Mention rate (how often the platform names you at all), citation share (how often a mention carries a link back to a page you own), position (where in the answer or source list you land) and share of answer (how much of the platform's text your source actually supports). A platform where you win on mention rate but lose on share of answer is a platform where you have brand recall and no distribution. A platform where you lose on mention rate but win on share of answer is where your content is being used efficiently. Both need different investments, and collapsing them into one metric is how teams end up optimising the wrong one.

11. Time to first citation: what to actually promise

Most GEO proposals promise visibility in weeks. Almost none of them specify which platform, and the number without a platform is meaningless. Here is the realistic curve.

Stage Typical duration What is happening
Crawl Hours to 48 hours (high-authority media) The crawler fetches the URL. Nothing else has happened yet.
Index admission 48 hours to 7 days (authority); 1–2 weeks (general) The page enters the candidate pool. It is retrievable, not yet selected.
First citation 2–7 days on fast platforms; 2–6 weeks on index-driven ones The platform chooses the page for at least one answer.
Cross-platform stability 3–6 months of sustained publishing Consistent citation across platforms, which is what attribution actually needs.
Durable citation Continuous Requires ongoing freshness. Citations are dynamic: hard-sell and filler content gets indexed, then purged on refresh.

The single most useful thing to tell a client is that indexing and citation are different events. Entering the candidate pool is a crawl achievement; being selected for an answer is a content achievement. A site can be indexed every week and never cited, and the usual causes are the four failure patterns covered in AI crawler access control and log analysis in 2026: blocked crawler, broken rendering, unanswered question, or absent corroboration.

The second most useful thing is to make the "3 to 6 months" figure explicit in the commercial plan, because it is the one that determines whether the programme survives. Platform selection churns monthly — forty to sixty percent of cited domains can change month to month — so single-month movement is noise. A programme that promises a quarterly outcome is honest; one that promises a fortnightly citation is not, and the difference shows up as churn in the client relationship rather than in the data.

12. How to pick your platform priorities

A comparison is only useful if it ends in a decision. Here is the framework I use, in order of weight.

Start with where your buyer already asks questions. This is the only criterion that should carry real weight. Consumer products live in ChatGPT. Enterprise and technical evaluation lives in Claude and Copilot. High-intent research and comparison shopping live in Perplexity. Chinese-language consumers live in Doubao and Yuanbao; Chinese-language technical and B2B buyers live in DeepSeek. The 2026 field interviews reinforce this: teams selling B2B or enterprise products often do better in Claude and Copilot than in ChatGPT, because that is where their buyers work all day, while consumer teams have the opposite profile.

Second, filter by procurement model against your existing assets. If your category is time-sensitive — travel, appliances, local services, sports, finance — you are in a Model A and Model D world, and the highest-leverage asset is ecosystem distribution rather than on-site craft. If your category is evergreen, authority-led and B2B, you are in a Model B and Model C world, and the highest-leverage assets are structured answer-shaped content, primary-source citation, named authors, and clean semantic HTML.

Third, filter by what you already own. If you rank well on Google, Google AI Overviews and Gemini are close to free. If you have Bing presence, Copilot is close to free. If you already publish on WeChat official accounts, Yuanbao is close to free. If you already publish on Toutiao and Douyin, Doubao is close to free. The cheapest citation is always the one that uses an asset you already maintain, and this is the step most comparison exercises skip because it is not about the platform.

Fourth, apply the reporting test. Rank the platforms you cannot measure last, not first. Two platforms — Google and Microsoft — give you first-party citation data and are therefore where you can run an experiment with a feedback loop. The rest require a manual prompt panel. If your team has no capacity to run a panel, your realistic platform list is two platforms, not seven, and that is a fine outcome.

Fifth, and finally, do not chase volume. Three well-covered platforms will beat seven badly covered ones, because coverage means publishing, measuring, and refreshing on each — and refreshing is where the compounding value lives. The topic-cluster architecture that supports this is set out in AI search topic clusters and content refresh in 2026, and the human-plus-AI production model that makes the refresh cadence affordable is in the AI and human content hybrid.

A defensible default for most teams in 2026. Western consumer or B2B: Google AI Overviews (because the ranking already exists), ChatGPT (because the audience is there), Perplexity (because it is fast and transparent), Claude (if enterprise). Chinese-language consumer: Doubao and Yuanbao. Chinese-language technical or B2B: DeepSeek and Tongyi. Add the fourth or fifth platform only after the first three are measurably working, and only if you have the refresh capacity to maintain them.

13. Eight comparison mistakes that cost a quarter

Having run this exercise for several years, the same eight errors account for most wasted GEO effort.

1. Treating the comparison table as a checklist. A table of preferences is a map of where sources come from, not a list of things to add to a page. "Perplexity favours freshness" does not translate into "add a date" — it translates into "publish something that did not exist six months ago, in a format that can be lifted cleanly". Checklists produce decorative work.

2. Confusing a citation-platform ranking with a share-of-answer ranking. Ranking a page for a query is not the same as being cited in an AI answer. The AI surface selects from a retrieved candidate set with different selection functions; a page can rank first and never be cited, particularly when it is a listicle without attributable claims.

3. Reading an absolute citation rate as a quality signal. Six citations out of fifty answers is not better than twelve out of twenty-five. Always pair the rate with the sample size and with the platform's overall citation density, otherwise you are comparing platforms using different denominators.

4. Testing each platform exactly once. DeepSeek cited on 9 of 12 questions in a single run and on 32 of 32 with search enabled. Claude answers without search on most queries by design. Single tests systematically under-count the platforms whose search is conditional. Sample properly before you conclude an engine "doesn't read the web".

5. Optimizing for the platform where you already appear. Confirmation bias is expensive in this field, because the platform you are good at is the platform whose rules you believe in. The correction is cheap: run one prompt panel per platform, including the ones you expect to fail, every month.

6. Publishing the same asset in every ecosystem. Doubao rewards conversational, conclusion-first FAQ shape. Claude rewards depth, primary sources and named authors. DeepSeek rewards technical long-form with reasoning structure. Pasting identical copy into all of them guarantees mediocre performance everywhere and strong performance nowhere. Adapt the format, keep the facts aligned.

7. Ignoring the enterprise switch. On Team and Enterprise plans, web search can be switched off at the workspace level, which means every employee gets training-data-only answers and your newest pages are invisible to them. If you sell to enterprises, the platform's product configuration is part of your distribution problem, not someone else's.

8. Declaring victory on one month of data. With forty to sixty percent of cited domains churning monthly, one good month is indistinguishable from noise. The decision cadence should be quarterly against a fixed prompt panel, with the panel itself reviewed monthly for drift.

14. A 90-day platform sequencing plan

Days 1–15 — decide and instrument. Name two to three platforms based on where your buyer asks questions, not where your brand is loudest. Set up the prompt panel for each, with ten to fifteen fixed questions drawn from real sales conversations, and run it three times per platform to get a variance baseline rather than a single data point. Fix access: allow the search and citation crawlers for every platform on your list, and confirm in server logs that they are actually arriving. If you have not done this, the debugging playbook in AI crawler access control and log analysis in 2026 will save you the quarter.

Days 16–45 — build the shared asset once. Choose one canonical page per priority cluster and rebuild it with the components every platform rewards: an answer block under the H1, self-contained section openers, comparison tables, an entity paragraph with consistent naming, a FAQ written in full sentences, visible dates with genuine updates, primary-source citations with dates, and captions carrying facts. Add Schema Markup for entity disambiguation, not as a citation switch. One page, many platforms, no duplicate URLs.

15. The one thing to take away

The comparison you have just read is not seven tips. Frequently asked questions

Which AI search platform is best to optimize for in 2026?

There is no best platform, only the platform your buyer uses. If you must sequence: start with Google AI Overviews if you already have Google rankings, because the AI layer inherits them; start with Perplexity if your category is time-sensitive, because it is the fastest to cite and the most transparent to monitor; start with ChatGPT if you sell to consumers; start with Claude if you sell to enterprises or technical buyers; start with Doubao if your market is Chinese-language consumer, and DeepSeek if it is Chinese-language technical. Optimizing for several platforms at once is fine, but only after the first one produces measurable citations — otherwise you are spreading a quarter of effort across seven learning curves.

Is ChatGPT or Perplexity better for getting citations?

For volume and speed, Perplexity. It averages 16.35 sources per answer against ChatGPT's 6.88, cites fresh content quickly, and shows you its sources so you can iterate. For influence per citation, ChatGPT. In the same 2026 study, ChatGPT's cited pages carried the highest average influence score — the pages it cites contribute more of the answer's substance. If your goal is referral traffic, Perplexity is the better earner. If your goal is being described accurately inside a synthesized answer, ChatGPT is the more valuable surface. Most teams need both, and the two reward different content properties: Perplexity rewards freshness and corroboration, ChatGPT rewards structure and entity clarity.

Do I need different content for Doubao, Yuanbao and DeepSeek?

You need the same facts in different shapes. The three domestic engines read largely different ecosystems — Toutiao and Douyin for Doubao, WeChat official accounts for Yuanbao, CSDN, Zhihu and news portals for DeepSeek — so a single unadapted asset can only ever be found by one of them. Keep the factual claims identical so cross-verification works, and change the format: conclusion-first conversational Q&A for Doubao, long-form authoritative interpretation for Yuanbao, technical long-form with parameters and reasoning structure for DeepSeek. Publishing identical copy into all three guarantees mediocre results in all three.

Does Claude require a different strategy from ChatGPT?

Yes, in three specific ways. Claude decides per query whether it needs the web at all, so brand recall and parameter memory matter more there than index presence. Claude's web search runs on Anthropic's own infrastructure rather than a resold index, so Bing or Google rankings do not transfer. And Claude rewards depth, primary sources, visible dates and named-author credentials more heavily than most engines. Operationally, the cheapest wins are verifying that Claude-SearchBot and Claude-User are allowed while ClaudeBot is blocked, and making sure your main content renders as plain HTML — Claude's web fetch tool does not run JavaScript.

How do I know which platform is actually citing me?

Two platforms publish first-party data for site owners: Google's Search Console Generative AI report, launched in June 2026, and Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report for Copilot. For the rest there is no dashboard, so the method is a fixed prompt panel: ten to fifteen questions your buyers actually ask, run on each platform, three times per platform, logged with mention rate, citation share, position and share of answer. Run it monthly and compare against the panel from two months earlier. Because cited domains churn forty to sixty percent month to month, one month's movement is not a trend.

Which platform should a small B2B brand prioritise?

Usually Claude and Copilot, ahead of ChatGPT. Enterprise buyers evaluate products inside Claude and Copilot during the working day, and Anthropic's citation behaviour favours the primary-source, named-author, deep-comparison content that technical B2B buyers respond to. Copilot may be the cheapest of all to win if you have any Bing presence. If your buyers are not enterprise, reverse the order and start with ChatGPT. If your market is Chinese-language B2B, start with DeepSeek, despite its slower indexing, because it is the broadest citation engine in that market and it over-indexes on exactly the technical content a B2B buyer wants.

How long before a new page gets cited?

For a high-authority publication on a fast platform, cite it within four to seven days, and sometimes within forty-eight hours on Perplexity. For a general site, expect the page to be crawled within days, admitted to the candidate pool in one to two weeks, and cited in two to six weeks. Cross-platform stability takes three to six months of sustained publishing. If you want a defensible commercial commitment, promise a quarterly outcome against a fixed prompt panel, not a fortnightly citation, because citation pools churn monthly and the platform that cited you in March may not cite you in June.

Does llms.txt or Schema Markup improve my citations?

Neither is a citation lever, and selling them as one is the most common way GEO budgets get wasted. Google's own material states there is no separate AI index and that llms.txt is not used in Search; controlled tests on Schema Markup found no citation effect that cleared the noise floor against large control groups. Publish llms.txt because it is cheap and states your priorities, and keep Schema Markup accurate because it helps entity disambiguation and interpretation. Neither should appear in a business case as a switch that turns citations on.

Is it worth optimizing for AI Overviews separately from Google Search?

No — they are the clearest case of one strategy covering both. AI Overviews draw their candidate set from Google's own ranked results, so Google SEO is the foundation and the AI layer is the extraction behaviour on top of it. Google has stated that no special markup is required for its AI features and that separate optimization is not needed. The one genuinely distinct investment for the AI layer is topic-cluster coverage, because Google's query fan-out retrieves a page once per matching subquery, and a page that covers the cluster gets more retrieval attempts than one covering only the head term.

My content is indexed by AI crawlers but never cited. What is wrong?

Index admission is a crawl achievement; citation is a content achievement, and four patterns explain most of the gap. The page may not answer the question in extractable form — buried in narrative, split across images, or rendered only after JavaScript. It may lack source authority, so it loses the twenty-five to thirty-five percent of citation probability that authority carries. It may be stale, which costs ten to fifteen percent and is fatal on freshness-led platforms. Or it may be uncorroborated, which matters enormously on Perplexity and across the domestic engines. The diagnosis procedure for all four is in AI crawler access control and log analysis in 2026.

Should I build one page for every AI platform?

No. One canonical URL with different emphasis inside it, per platform. Duplicate pages per platform split your own authority, create duplicate-content problems, and dilute the entity signals that matter most on parameter-first platforms like ChatGPT and Claude. What genuinely justifies platform-specific versions is ecosystem distribution, where the content must live inside someone else's platform to be retrieved at all — a WeChat official account, a Toutiao account, a Douyin video. Those are separate distribution channels, not duplicate pages of your own site.

The lagging layer is revenue reconciled against server logs rather than analytics session attribution alone.
