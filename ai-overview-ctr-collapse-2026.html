AI Overview CTR Collapse in 2026: How to Diagnose the 58% Click Loss and What to Do

By Long Yaren · Published 29 October 2026 · 14 min read

Bottom line: If your organic traffic fell while your rankings held, you did not lose a ranking war. You lost a click war, and the weapon was a search feature. The single most useful measurement published in 2026 is a December 2025 Ahrefs dataset of 300,000 keywords showing that the presence of an AI Overview correlates with a 58% lower click-through rate for the position-one page — up from the 34.5% the same team measured a year earlier on a comparable sample (Ahrefs, April 2025). This article is the diagnostic playbook I use with clients: how to prove that a traffic decline is an AI Overview click collapse rather than a ranking loss, how to read the Search Console graph that shows it, and where to build demand when the answer engine absorbs the click.

It deliberately does not repeat the rest of this site's advice about earning citations. The question here is narrower and earlier in the funnel: is the traffic I am losing even recoverable by ranking better? Often the answer is no, and knowing that earlier saves a quarter of wasted effort.

The magnitude of the collapse, in numbers you can audit

Two studies from the same dataset family, run a year apart, let you measure how fast this is moving. Both use aggregated Google Search Console data — summed clicks over summed impressions, so the CTR is volume-weighted rather than an average of averages — and both compare keywords that trigger an AI Overview against informational keywords that do not.

Two methodological details matter, and both are the kind of thing that gets lost when these numbers get quoted second-hand.

First, the forecast is a counterfactual, not a measurement. The 34.5% and 58% figures are produced by taking the observed CTR drop on informational keywords that do not trigger an AI Overview, and using it to predict what the AI Overview keywords' CTR would have been in a world without AI Overviews. The arithmetic in the February 2026 update is explicit: the forecast is 0.072627 multiplied by (0.038943 divided by 0.075730), and the loss is (0.015677 minus 0.03734) divided by 0.03734. This is a sound way to isolate the feature's effect, but it means the number is an estimate of an unobserved state, not a measurement of a controlled A/B test. Treat it as directional and well-founded, not as a physical constant.

Second, the sample is deliberately skewed, and that is a strength. The April 2025 study selected 300,000 keywords from Ahrefs' Keywords Explorer database: 150,000 with an AI Overview present and 150,000 informational keywords without one. The same team found that 99.2% of keywords that trigger AI Overviews are informational in intent, so restricting the control group to informational intent is what makes the two samples comparable at all. It also tells you something important about who is affected: the collapse lands on exactly the queries — definitions, how-tos, comparisons, background explainers — that most content marketing has been built to capture.

The February 2026 update also broke the impact down by position, which is the most operationally useful table in either study. AI Overviews do not only hurt position one; they compress the entire result page.

The practical consequence: if your strategy is "rank in the top ten and harvest the long tail", the compression is smaller at the bottom of the page than at the top — but the traffic you were harvesting from positions six to ten was always the smallest part of the click stack.

The same update notes that around 9% of AI Overviews appear outside position one in the SERP, and that the negative impact continues at those lower positions in less dramatic terms. The Ahrefs authors also point to corroborating research from other firms — via their own write-up, Seer Interactive reporting organic CTR down between 49.4% and 65.2%, Kevin Indig above 50%, Authoritas at 47.5%, and the Daily Mail reporting 80–90% lower CTR. Those are secondary reports of other people's studies, not the same measurement, so I use them as a range of corroboration rather than as additional instances of the same figure.

A limit worth stating plainly: none of these studies measures what a single site lost. They measure population-level CTR shifts across keyword sets, and they use desktop Search Console data. Your site's exposure depends on which of its queries trigger AI Overviews, which varies by geography, device and intent. Do not multiply your impressions by 58% and call it a forecast — that is the single most common misreading of this dataset, and it produces panic rather than a plan.

What users actually do when an AI Overview is present

Correlation studies describe the aggregate. Behavioural data describes the mechanism, and the mechanism is what lets you decide whether a click is recoverable.

Pew Research Center (22 July 2025) analysed browsing data from 900 US adults who agreed to have their activity tracked, covering 68,879 unique Google searches performed between 1 and 31 March 2025, of which 12,593 produced an AI summary. Results were collected by scraping the first page of results between 7 and 17 April 2025. The behavioural findings:

  • Users who saw an AI summary clicked a traditional search result link in 8% of visits; users who did not see one clicked in 15% of visits.
  • Users who saw an AI summary clicked a link inside the summary in just 1% of visits.
  • Users ended their browsing session entirely on 26% of pages with an AI summary, against 16% of pages with only traditional results.
  • Around 58% of respondents conducted at least one Google search in March 2025 that produced an AI-generated summary.

The 1% figure is the one that should reshape your strategy. If being cited inside the AI Overview were a meaningful traffic source, the citation link would earn a meaningful share of clicks. It earns one percent. The citation is still worth having — it is an authority and relevance signal that feeds models and can compound — but for traffic purposes, being cited inside an AI answer is close to a rounding error.

Google's own position on the topic is worth reading directly, because it is narrower than the market's fear-mongering and considerably more useful. Google's Search Central documentation on AI features states that "there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary," and that "you don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features." Eligibility is simply that the page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet. There is no "AI schema" to add, and no llms.txt to ship. Google's guidance is that the existing SEO best practices remain the whole job.

What this means strategically: the collapse is not caused by anything you did wrong technically, and there is no technical patch. If you were waiting for a fix — a schema attribute, a crawler allowlist, a special file — the honest answer is that no such lever has been documented by Google or demonstrated by any study.

The decoupling: impressions up, clicks down

The pattern that shows up in Search Console is distinctive enough to be diagnostic on its own. Ahrefs' analysis of its own blog (17 June 2025) documents it: over the final six months of 2024, the daily impressions and clicks of the Ahrefs blog had a positive correlation of 0.425. Over the first six months of 2025 that correlation inverted to −0.352. Impressions and clicks had decoupled — one climbs while the other falls.

Two things are happening at once, and confusing them leads to bad decisions.

On the impressions side: Ahrefs found that alongside the March core update, the presence of AI Overviews doubled — increasing by 116% — and the same team found over 19,000 keywords that trigger an AI Overview and include Ahrefs in the top ten. AI Overviews also use a "query fan-out" technique, issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources to build a response, which surfaces a wider and more diverse set of supporting links than a classic web search (Google). More of your pages appear. Good.

On the clicks side: the same feature resolves the query on the results page, so fewer people leave it. Your rankings hold; your traffic does not.

The trap: a ranking collapse and a click collapse look identical on a traffic chart. If you diagnose "traffic fell" as "we lost rankings" and respond by rewriting content, tightening internal links and chasing links to the same informational keywords, you will spend a quarter pushing a page that was never going to receive the click — because the click was never available.

The diagnostic: separating click loss from ranking loss

This is the procedure. It is designed to be run on a single afternoon with a Search Console export and a rank tracker, and it produces one of three verdicts: ranking loss, click loss, or mixed.

Step 1 — Split the decline by query intent

Export Search Console performance data for the comparison window and the baseline window. Split by query group:

  • Informational — definitions, how-tos, "what is", "how to".
  • Commercial investigation — "best", "vs", "top X for Y", "alternatives to".
  • Transactional — pricing, "buy", "near me", product and brand-modifier queries.
  • Brand — queries containing your brand or product name.

Then check the shape of the decline per group. In the data I have seen, the signature of an AI Overview click collapse is informational and commercial-investigation traffic down, transactional and brand traffic roughly flat. A ranking loss looks different: it hits every group that shared the affected pages, and it correlates with position changes rather than with query type.

Step 2 — Check whether position held while clicks fell

For the same queries, compare average position across the two windows. If position is flat or improving while clicks per impression dropped, the ranking was never the problem. The single metric that carries this is clicks divided by impressions on the affected queries, segmented by position one versus positions two to ten. Ahrefs' position-level table predicts the shape of what you will find: a steeper position-one CTR decline than tail decline.

Step 3 — Quantify the exposure

Determine what share of your total organic clicks sits on queries that trigger an AI Overviews. This is the number that decides whether you are in a crisis or a managed decline. If AI Overview-triggering queries account for a large share of your clicks, the 58% figure is the relevant ceiling on your recoverable traffic. If they account for a small share, you have a different problem and should not spend your quarter on this one.

Step 4 — Verify against behavioural baselines

Cross-check your measured position-one CTR against what is achievable in your category. Ahrefs' December 2025 control sample — informational keywords without AI Overviews — sat at a position-one CTR of 0.039, while the AI Overview keywords sat at 0.016. If your measured position-one CTR is near 4%, you are losing nothing to AI Overviews. If it is near 1.6%, the feature has already taken most of what it can.

Step 5 — Check that you are actually eligible

Confirm the affected pages are indexed and eligible to show a snippet, that crawling is allowed in robots.txt and by any CDN, that important content is available in textual form, and that structured data matches the visible text. These are the mechanical requirements Google lists for appearing as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode (Google). They are also exactly the failures that produce an apparent click collapse, and they are fixable — unlike the feature itself.

Verdict template: "Between window A and window B, position-one CTR on AI Overview-triggering informational queries fell from X to Y while average position held at Z. N percent of organic clicks sit on AI Overview-triggering queries. Control-group CTR for non-AIO informational queries was V, so the gap attributable to the feature is G points. Verdict: click loss, not ranking loss. Recoverable by ranking: none for the affected queries. Action: demand-side plan."

Reading four shapes of Search Console decline

Not every traffic dip is an AI Overview event. Four shapes show up repeatedly, and they have different causes and different fixes.

ShapeWhat you seeVerdictWhat to do
The crocodile Impressions climbing, clicks falling, position flat. The correlation inverts from positive to negative. Click loss. This is the signature case in Ahrefs' decoupling analysis. Stop optimising for the click. Rebuild demand off-site and in branded and transactional queries.
The step down Sudden, single-day drop across all intents and positions, coinciding with a confirmed algorithm update. Ranking loss. Classic update response. Compare against the update's scope and audit the affected templates; see the March 2026 core update retrospective.
The seasonal wedge A steady drift in the same direction across the whole year, with no step and no inflection. Usually not a feature event. Check seasonality, competitive SERP changes and index coverage first. Run the index-coverage and cannibalisation audit before touching content strategy.
The eligibility gap Some pages fall, others hold, and the split follows a template or a section rather than a topic. Technical. Crawling, indexing or snippet eligibility. Work through the five eligibility checks in Step 5, then re-measure after two weeks.

Four responses, and the one that does not work

Once you have confirmed a click collapse, four responses are defensible. One common response is not.

1 — Shift the mix toward queries that still earn clicks

Transactional and navigational intent still click. A query like "buy", "pricing", "near me", or a brand-modifier query is a request to act, not to be informed, and the AI Overview does not satisfy the intent. The Pew data supports the mechanism: users who got an AI summary still ended their session 26% of the time against 16% without one, meaning most of them did continue browsing — but on their own terms, at their own pace.

The practical version of this is not "write more bottom-funnel content". It is to find the queries where you already rank and where the intent requires arrival, and to move your best internal links, your most linked pages and your call-to-action placements toward them. You are reallocating internal authority from queries that will not click to queries that will.

2 — Accept the loss on informational queries and profit from the citation

A cited informational page that gets one percent of the summary's clicks can still be a compounding asset: the citation is a signal that models and search systems use, and it keeps the URL attached to the topic. The play is to keep publishing for these queries — as reference material for machines and for the small share of users who do click — while changing what success means for them from sessions to citations and share of voice. That shift is the subject of AI citation tracking in 2026.

3 — Build the off-site presence that decides AI visibility

Ahrefs' 75,000-brand correlation study (26 May 2025) is the strongest evidence available on which signals track AI visibility. Branded web mentions correlate at 0.664 with appearing in AI Overviews, against 0.218 for backlink count, 0.326 for Domain Rating and 0.295 for referring domain count. In the same study, 26% of brands had no mentions at all, and brands earning the most web mentions saw up to 10 times more mentions in AI Overviews than the next quartile.

A later Ahrefs dataset added the channel dimension: YouTube mentions correlate at about 0.737 with AI Overviews mentions, against roughly 0.717 for YouTube mention impressions and 0.66 to 0.71 for branded web mentions depending on the engine, while content volume on your own site correlates at only about 0.194. Correlations are not causation and Ahrefs says so plainly, but the ranking of these signals is itself the strategic message: what makes you visible to an AI answer is largely what other people say about you off your own site, not what you publish on it.

4 — Own a surface the answer engine cannot absorb

Some destinations cannot be summarised. A pricing calculator, a configurator, a comparison tool, a free utility, a private community, a live event, a downloadable dataset — these are destinations a user must travel to, not read about. Google calls this out indirectly in its own framing: an AI Overview is only shown when it adds something beyond the traditional results, which is why a page whose value is "do this" rather than "know this" is structurally less substitutable.

The response that does not work: chasing the same informational keywords harder

If you already rank on the affected queries and the click is gone, more content on the same topic produces more impressions of the same kind and the same flat CTR. You will report more impressions, more citations and roughly the same revenue. This is the most common way teams respond to the collapse, and it works well enough to look like progress for two quarters while the business quietly stops growing. The diagnostic in Step 1 exists precisely to catch this: if the affected queries are informational, ranking harder is not the answer.

Rebuilding demand where an AI answer cannot take it

Everything above is subtraction — protecting the clicks that remain. This section is addition, and it is where most of the recoverable value sits.

Make branded search the leading indicator

Brand and direct traffic is the least compressible line in the report. If impressions are up and clicks are down, a rise in branded queries and direct traffic is the counter-signal that your citation surface is still working — people saw the answer, and some of them went looking for you by name. Track branded query volume separately from non-branded, weekly, and treat the ratio between them as your citation-to-demand conversion rate.

Instrument the AI referral channel properly

Google Search Central states that AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic is counted in the Performance report under the "Web" search type, alongside traditional results. That means AI Overview traffic and classic organic traffic are largely fused in your reporting — you cannot separate them from the Performance report alone. If you need the split, you need a second layer: a distinct analytics channel grouping for AI assistant referrers, and a branded-query segment. The measurement design for that layer is covered in measuring GEO ROI and attribution.

Invest in the mentions, not only the pages

The correlation data above points at a specific, under-served activity: getting your brand and its claims mentioned by third parties, including in video, which is the single strongest correlate in Ahrefs' later dataset. In practice this means original research that other people want to quote, expert commentary that publishers want to run, and a genuine presence in the places where your buyers already talk. A 30 / 60 / 90-day response plan

Days 1–30 — Diagnose and stop the bleeding

  • Run the five-step diagnostic. Produce the verdict template with real numbers, not adjectives.
  • Quantify the share of organic clicks sitting on AI Overview-triggering queries. This is the number everything else is scaled against.
  • Complete the five eligibility checks on the worst-hit templates.
  • Stop publishing net-new content for the affected informational clusters until the diagnosis is done. Publishing into a zero-click query set is the single most expensive mistake available here.
  • Segment reporting so AI assistant referrals and branded queries are visible separately from the start.

Days 31–60 — Reallocate

  • Move internal link equity and conversion placements from zero-click informational pages toward transactional and commercial-investigation pages that already rank.
  • Refresh the pages in the top decile of impressions but bottom decile of clicks — they are the ones AI Overviews have absorbed, and refreshing them improves the citation surface even if the click never returns.
  • Begin the off-site mentions programme. Pick the third-party surface where your buyers already read, and target one substantive mention per week rather than one hundred directory listings.
  • Rewrite the measurement reporting so that sessions is not the headline metric for the affected cluster. Citations and branded queries are the metrics now.

Days 61–90 — Build the non-substitutable surface

  • Launch or extend one destination that cannot be answered in a paragraph: a calculator, a free tool, a dataset, a template, a community.
  • Convert your best informational asset into a durable citation asset — with the original data, the named expert and the explicit claim that an AI can lift.
  • Re-run the diagnostic on a trailing window. The comparison you want is not "did traffic recover" — it is "did the CTR gap between AIO and non-AIO queries narrow, and did branded and direct traffic rise while informational clicks held steady".

Frequently asked questions

Is a 58% CTR drop real or is it marketing?

It is a real measurement of a real population, with two caveats. It is derived from aggregated desktop Search Console data across 300,000 keywords, and the headline number is a counterfactual forecast rather than a controlled test. The underlying CTR figures — 0.073 down to 0.016 for AI Overview position-one keywords, against a control group moving from 0.076 to 0.039 — are directly observed. The gap between them is the inference. Both Ahrefs studies are published with their methodology, and the independent behavioural work from Pew points the same direction.

Should I try to opt out of AI Overviews?

Google's own documentation states that there are no additional requirements or special optimizations needed to appear in AI features, which implies there is no documented opt-out mechanism for AI Overviews specifically. What you can control is whether AI crawlers may fetch your content, and blocking them reduces your chances of being cited or of appearing as a supporting link. The trade-off is worth making explicitly per site, not by default.

Does being cited in an AI Overview bring traffic?

Very little directly. Pew's data shows users clicked a link inside an AI summary in 1% of visits. The citation's value is upstream of traffic: it is an authority and relevance signal, and it feeds the models that decide what gets recommended. If your business case depends on sessions from AI Overviews, that case will not hold.

How do I know if my traffic drop is AI Overviews or an algorithm update?

Three checks, in order: does the drop correlate with query intent (informational hit hardest) rather than with templates; did position hold while clicks fell; and is there a confirmed event in Google's status dashboard on the date of the step. A ranking loss shows position changes. A click loss shows CTR changes with position intact.

Is this bad for SEO overall?

Different for different layers. Traffic from informational queries is compressed and the traffic is largely gone. Authority signals from being cited are not gone, and the same 75,000-brand dataset suggests off-site mentions and video presence correlate more strongly with AI visibility than on-site content volume does. The strategic shift is real: less of a page-production game, more of a distribution and entity game.

What is the one number I should put in front of my client?

The share of your organic clicks that sit on queries triggering an AI Overview, and the position-one CTR on those queries versus the same queries' position-one CTR a year ago. Those two numbers decide whether this is a crisis, a managed decline, or noise — and they decide whether any of the above plan is worth running.
