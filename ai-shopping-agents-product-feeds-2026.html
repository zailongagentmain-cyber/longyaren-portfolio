Short version (Oct 2026): The checkout layer retreated — OpenAI handed ChatGPT checkout back to merchants in March 2026 and now competes on discovery; Anthropic's reference agent cannot place an order; Google keeps the retailer as merchant of record. So the job is not "sell inside the agent". It is "be legible to the agent, then convert on your own site".

Every GEO article on the market is about being cited. Agentic commerce is a different mechanism: an AI shopping agent does not quote you, it lists you — and it makes that decision by reading machine-readable product data, not by reading your marketing copy. That distinction changes what you have to fix, and it changes what you should measure.

This is the state of the channel as of late October 2026, with every number attached to its sample size, date and caveats.

The checkout layer retreated — discovery did not

The market narrative for 2025 was that AI shopping agents would become the checkout. In 2026, the major platforms pulled in the opposite direction.

In March 2026 OpenAI wrote that its Instant Checkout "did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide, so we're allowing merchants to use their own checkout experiences while we focus our efforts on product discovery" (OpenAI, 24 Mar 2026). The same post describes Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) being extended "to be the connective layer between merchants and users throughout discovery", with merchants sharing product feeds and promotions "so their catalogs are fully represented in ChatGPT".

The other platforms went the same way. Anthropic's reference agent was built so that "no method places an order or moves money", according to its own source code, and Google's UCP developer guide keeps the retailer in charge: "You remain the Merchant of Record" (both quoted in AI Velocity, 10 Oct 2026). Google announced UCP in January 2026 with more than 20 ecosystem backers including Adyen, Mastercard, Stripe and Visa, covering "the entire shopping journey — from discovery and buying to post-purchase support" (Google, 11 Jan 2026).

Why this matters for positioning: you are not being asked to re-platform onto a marketplace that will own your customer. You are being asked to make your catalog legible to a new selection layer, and then to convert the traffic on a checkout you already control. The merchant-of-record model also means your ROI numbers stay your own — which is the same argument I make in measuring AI citation share.

One more ranking detail worth knowing, because it is not the same as SEO ranking: according to Parse, "ChatGPT explicitly states product results are not ads and not influenced by partnerships, and that merchants for the same product are ranked on availability, price, quality, primary-seller status, and whether Instant Checkout is enabled" (Parse, 2026, citing OpenAI). Note what is absent: keyword match, topical authority, content quality in the editorial sense. Availability, price, quality, seller status — and feed plumbing.

Where brands actually lose: discovery, selection, checkout

The most useful framing I found is a staged one, from eCommerceInsights: "The practical question for an ecommerce lead is not 'is the future agentic' but 'which stage is live, and what work pays off at every stage regardless of which protocol wins'" (eCommerceInsights, 10 Jun 2026). Stage one — AI shopping research — is fully live; draft carts and agent-completed checkout are later stages, and the work that pays off across all of them is protocol-agnostic data work.

The demand-side numbers explain why. Consumer willingness to delegate is far higher than willingness to delegate money:

The same pattern shows up in what shoppers do before they commit — 93% of AI-assisted shoppers look for evidence outside the AI before ordering, and 94% verify what the AI said (Sanbi.ai, 23 Oct 2026, citing Attentive). That is why the landing experience still matters: the agent shortlists, the human verifies, and your site closes.

Your product feed is the ranking layer

If selection runs on machine-readable fields, the feed is not a technical chore — it is the ranking surface. OpenAI's product feed specification is the most concrete public definition we have: "OpenAI's feed specification lists nine required fields: item_id, title, description, url, brand, seller_name, image_url, availability and price. It accepts JSONL or Google-compatible tab or comma-delimited files. It sets no fixed refresh interval; it tells merchants to keep price and availability current and to update the feed when a sale starts or ends" (AI Velocity, 10 Oct 2026).

Nine required fields, no refresh schedule, and an explicit instruction to keep availability and price current. That is the whole contract, and almost every catalogue I would audit is failing at least one clause of it.

The eligibility consequence is blunt: "A merchant with a clean, current, attribute-rich feed is eligible across all four agents in the table. A merchant with a perfect checkout integration and a thin feed is eligible for none of the shortlists that lead to it" (AI Velocity). Checkout integration without feed data buys you nothing, because the shortlist is decided upstream of checkout.

And the share of shopping answers that come from feed data is moving fast. Retention Edge, citing Profound's tracking, reports "61.54% Share of tracked ChatGPT Shopping recommendations drawn from merchant feeds on July 10, 2026, up from 8.26% a day earlier" (Retention Edge, 8 Oct 2026). I would treat that day-over-day swing as a warning rather than a trend line — a one-day change that large is more likely to reflect a methodology or panel change than a real overnight shift — but the direction is the point: the agent's answer set is increasingly assembled from merchant feeds.

Trans-cited, flagged: the 61.54% and 8.26% figures are Profound's tracking as relayed by Retention Edge; I have not seen the underlying dataset. Use them as directional evidence about feed-sourced recommendations, not as a benchmark to plan against. If you need a number you own, track your own visibility across engines rather than borrowing someone else's panel.

There is one population that can ignore most of this: merchants on Shopify. OpenAI states that "For merchants on Shopify, product data is already integrated into ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog... No additional work is required from individual merchants" (OpenAI, 24 Mar 2026). If you run a self-hosted or custom stack, feed hygiene is your job — and it is the job with the highest leverage in this entire channel.

Attributes beat prose

The agents compare products on specifics. Retention Edge summarises it as: "Agents compare products on specifics: materials, dimensions, compatibility, sizing and use cases. Fill in every attribute your feeds support and use accurate identifiers such as GTINs. Google also invites merchants to submit 'conversational attributes' that add richer context to listings" (Retention Edge, 8 Oct 2026).

Google announced the same direction in January 2026: "we're announcing dozens of new data attributes in Merchant Center designed for easy discovery in the conversational commerce era, on surfaces like AI Mode, Gemini and Business Agent. These new attributes complement retailers' existing data feeds and go beyond traditional keywords to include things like answers to common product questions, compatible accessories or substitutes" (Google, 11 Jan 2026). Conversational attributes — material, compatibility, use case — are the fields that let an agent answer "will this work with X" without a human.

One caveat on the adoption number attached to those attributes: a joint Google and lululemon test found roughly 50% of merchant-submitted conversational attributes were adopted into relevant AI Mode recommendations — a single brand, a single engine, no published sample size. Treat it as evidence that the field works, not as a target.

Accuracy is the other half. Stripe's guidance, cited by Retention Edge, is to keep "the share of agent checkouts that fail because an item is unavailable below 5%" — an internal recommendation value rather than a measured statistic, but the mechanism is obvious: an agent that tries to buy something out of stock creates a failed order, and a failed order is a lost customer, not a retry.

And the source mix of shopping citations is not the mix of ordinary AI answers. Azoma's analysis of 38 million citations found "editorial content is 51.9% of what Gemini cites for shopping, and affiliate content 39.6% of what Alexa for Shopping cites" (Sanbi.ai, 23 Oct 2026, citing Azoma). Gift guides, reviews and comparisons carry weight in this channel — which is a content brief, not a schema patch, and it is the same conclusion I reached about entity and third-party proof in entity SEO.

AI-referred traffic converts better

The channel is small but the traffic it brings is unusually good. Two things are true at once: the absolute share of AI-referred traffic is still under half a percent, and its quality is far above average. That combination argues for a specific order of operations — instrument the AI referral segment you already have, get the economics right, then invest in agentic plumbing. It does not argue for a replatforming decision this quarter.

Trust is a data problem, not a copy problem

Brands treat agentic commerce as a channel problem. The 2026 evidence says it is first a data-quality problem, because models describe brands incorrectly. Harvard Business Review's Acar and Schweidel report that "LLM data was often incomplete or incorrect. One popular AI model, for instance, miscategorized Ballantine's Scotch whiskey, an affordable mass-market offering, as a prestige product" (HBR, Mar–Apr 2026). Their framing of why it matters: two-thirds of Gen Zers and more than half of Millennials had already started using LLMs to research products, so "the share of model" — how models describe you — becomes a measurable asset.

Consistency across surfaces is the remediation. Martech Publishers put it as: "A brand selling skincare products, for example, should ensure that its product claims, ingredients, product descriptions, reviews, educational content, and third-party references are consistent. Inconsistent information creates uncertainty. Consistency creates confidence" (Martech Publishers, 8 Sep 2026). That is the same standard I apply to machine-readable facts — one version of the truth, stated in a form a machine can carry.

There is also a counter-intuitive opening. McKinsey and BoF's State of Fashion 2026 notes that "larger brands are less represented in AI assistants compared to challenger brands" (Qwairy, 2026, relaying the report). For challengers and mid-market brands, agentic discovery is a way to be represented against incumbents whose brand equity is not automatically inherited by the model.

Measure two things, not one

The most reusable idea in this whole channel is the split between visibility and position, and between agent-readability and citation. ShopOS, summarising Parcel Perform's AI Visibility Index, notes that "visibility also differs from ranking position, meaning that being mentioned by an AI system does not necessarily mean appearing as one of its most prominent recommendations" (ShopOS, 19 Sep 2026). If you track mentions only, you will systematically overestimate your position.

eCommerceInsights makes the second split explicit: "For the merchant, the qualifying bar is machine-readability: accurate price and availability in Product JSON-LD, parseable variant structure, and discoverable shipping and returns policies. A PDP that converts humans can still be unreadable to the agent doing the drafting — that gap is what the agent-readability score measures, separately from citation."

So a workable measurement stack has three layers, and none of them replaces the others:

  • Feed readiness — required fields present, identifiers valid, price and availability current. Binary, and fully under your control.
  • Agent visibility — how often you appear in shopping answers, and at what position. Needs a fixed panel of prompts, because visibility varies sharply between brands even inside one category.
  • AI-referred commerce — sessions, conversion and revenue per visit from AI referrals, segmented separately from other channels.

One advantage of instrumenting early is attribution. Parse argues that "agentic commerce attribution will be cleaner than AI-referral attribution in regular search, because the buy happens inside the agent surface and the protocols carry transaction identifiers" — so the medium-term payoff of proper tracking is a cleaner revenue line than the AI referral mess in ordinary search. I would still instrument on your own side first; see my GEO ROI and attribution guide for why third-party panels keep disagreeing with bank data.

Read the numbers carefully

Three cautions, because most of this channel's public numbers are forecasts, relays or vendor-built indices.

Scale estimates disagree by an order of magnitude. Parse summarises the spread: "McKinsey's $3–5 trillion is a 2030 number, not a 2026 number. Morgan Stanley estimates the more conservative slice at $190–385 billion in U.S. e-commerce by 2030, and Bain at $300–500 billion" (Parse, 2026). If a vendor pitches you on a market-size number, ask which of these they are using and what year it refers to.

Much of the underlying evidence is relayed. The Adobe, L.E.K., Attentive, PayPal, Salesforce, Deloitte and Azoma figures above are all second-hand as I received them — from Sanbi.ai's October tracker or from Retention Edge, which in turn cites Modern Retail, Amazon's Q2 2026 results and Profound. Where I could not reach the primary page, the source list says so. That is not a reason to ignore the numbers; it is a reason to treat them as directional and to measure your own.

Vendor indices are not benchmarks. Parcel Perform's AI Visibility Index reports brand-level visibility scores (Levi's 77.8%, Calvin Klein 55.6%, CeraVe 44.5% in the version ShopOS quotes) without disclosing the prompt set or sample size. A score you cannot reproduce is a score you cannot act on. Use the shape of the metric, not the number.

And the sane default advice for anyone being sold an agentic commerce suite: "Do not re-platform for a pilot. Do not sign a multi-year 'agentic commerce suite' contract to solve a problem that is currently a robots.txt edit and a schema sweep. And do not report agent-completed checkout revenue to your board as if it were a current channel; as of mid-2026 it is not" (eCommerceInsights).

A 30-day agent-readability checklist

  1. Audit the feed against the nine required fields — item_id, title, description, url, brand, seller_name, image_url, availability, price. Fix the gaps that block listing, not the ones that look nice.
  2. Add identifiers — GTINs where you have them, stable item_id values that will not churn between feeds.
  3. Publish conversational attributes — material, dimensions, compatibility, sizing, use case, compatible accessories and substitutes. This is where the agent answers the question a human would ask.
  4. Fix price and availability accuracy — target the Stripe threshold of under 5% of agent checkouts failing on unavailability. Out-of-stock errors are lost orders, not retries.
  5. Make the policy layer machine-readable — shipping and returns discoverable in Product JSON-LD, not only in prose.
  6. Check your entity consistency — the same product claim, the same ingredients, the same story across your site, your listings and third-party references. Model miscategorisation starts with inconsistency.
  7. Segment AI referrals in analytics — you cannot size this channel if it is not a separate segment. Do this before any tooling conversation.
  8. Build a shopping prompt panel — 30 to 50 fixed queries across your categories, run monthly, tracking mention and position separately. Reuse the measurement discipline from AI citation tracking; this channel is the same problem wearing a commerce hat.

None of these eight items requires a new platform, a new agency or a contract. They require the same discipline you already apply to structured data and third-party proof — which is why the teams that win in AI search will win here too.

FAQ

Will AI shopping agents take over my checkout?

Not in 2026. OpenAI stepped back from ChatGPT checkout in March 2026, Anthropic's reference agent cannot move money, and Google keeps the retailer as merchant of record. Only 8% to 10% of AI-assisted purchases complete inside a chatbot, while 60% of shoppers prefer the seller's own site. Plan for "selected by the agent, purchased on your site".

Is product data the same as SEO content?

No. The nine required feed fields, the attributes and the identifiers are structured product data; the ranking signals that drive AI citations are editorial and third-party. Both matter, they are maintained by different teams, and they fail differently. This is the split eCommerceInsights calls agent-readability versus citation.

Should I build a brand agent?

Eventually, and Google is making it purchasable: Gemini Enterprise for Customer Experience "is built on open standards and is already fully integrated with the Universal Commerce Protocol" (Google, 11 Jan 2026). But do not start there. Feed readiness and measurement come first — an agent cannot recommend what it cannot read, and you cannot improve what you do not measure.

How big is this really?

Small today, and moving fast: AI referrals are under 0.5% of inbound traffic on L.E.K.'s framing but growing at a 176% CAGR, and Adobe's holiday forecast puts 2026 AI-referred retail traffic up 130%. The 2030 projections range from $190 billion to $5 trillion depending on whose model you use, which is another way of saying nobody knows yet.

Is this just GEO with a different name?

Overlapping, not identical. GEO asks whether an AI engine will cite you in an answer; agentic commerce asks whether an AI agent will list you, shortlist you and hand you a customer who converts. The mechanism is data, not editorial authority — which is why the feed is the ranking layer, and why the work is unglamorous and high-leverage.
